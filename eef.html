00 // Governing objective

Preserve viability and the capacity to create future value.

The system does not need to preserve its current structure. It needs to preserve the essential capabilities that let it continue fulfilling its purpose.

Core idea: Survival through adaptation and repurposing — not preservation of every existing component.

Entropy Engine treats order, simplicity, speed, profit, and reuse as means rather than unconditional goals. Each must justify itself against the purpose and long-term viability of the whole system.

02 // Foundational principle

Survival through repurposing

Survival Principle: Preserve the system's capacity to continue fulfilling its essential purpose by maintaining critical resources, adapting to changing conditions, and repurposing useful potential when existing arrangements no longer work.
  • Survival is not the preservation of the current structure indefinitely.
  • Short-term output must not systematically destroy long-term capacity.
  • Repurpose useful potential before discarding it, when reuse is viable.
  • Change the purpose, structure, or business model when evidence shows the current arrangement is no longer viable.

Repurposing is a preference, not a command. If reuse is unsafe, infeasible, or more costly than a viable alternative, release the resource responsibly.

03 // Conflict resolution

The decision constitution

Use these rules when pillars conflict. The order is a default sequence of consideration, not a universal formula for every ethical or business trade-off.

RULE 01

Protect hard constraints

Respect legal obligations, critical safety limits, contractual commitments, and minimum viability thresholds.

RULE 02

Protect future viability

Account for cash, capacity, maintenance, people, knowledge, and the resources needed to continue operating.

RULE 03

Pursue intended value

Evaluate choices against the system's purpose, beneficiaries, and external dependencies.

RULE 04

Choose sufficient action

Compare lifecycle cost, risk, opportunity cost, complexity, and expected benefit.

RULE 05

Preserve options

Prefer bounded, reversible experiments under uncertainty when appropriate. Compare repurposing with replacement, retirement, and inaction.

RULE 06

Verify and reconsider

Assign an owner, define expected results, specify reconsideration triggers, and change course when evidence changes.

Conflict rule: When legitimate objectives conflict, make the trade-off explicit, identify who bears the cost, and assign responsibility for the decision.
04 // Decision economics

Reduce uncertainty only when it is worth the cost

Not all uncertainty is harmful. Some is tolerable; some creates opportunity. Entropy is a guiding metaphor here, not a universal organizational quantity or a claim that all uncertainty can be measured as physical entropy.

V(a) = B(a) − C(a) − R(a) − O(a)
  • B(a) — expected benefit of action a.
  • C(a) — total cost: implementation, operation, verification, maintenance, and failure handling.
  • R(a) — expected risk penalty, including downside exposure.
  • O(a) — opportunity cost of committed resources.

This is a conceptual decision aid, not a precise universal calculation. Use ranges, scenarios, and explicit assumptions when exact measurement is not possible. Include the value of information when an experiment can improve a later decision.

05 // Resource allocation

Sustainable circulation

The goal is neither to distribute everything nor to accumulate everything. It is to preserve a viable balance between obligations, renewal, resilience, rewards, and future opportunity.

1

Meet obligations and essential operating costs

Keep the system legally, contractually, and operationally viable.

↓
2

Maintain and replenish

Restore infrastructure, skills, working capacity, knowledge, and other critical inputs.

↓
3

Maintain appropriate resilience

Hold reserves and spare capacity proportionate to uncertainty and potential shocks.

↓
4

Distribute agreed rewards

Use explicit rules that account for contributions, responsibility, agreements, rights, and fairness.

↓
5

Invest in validated opportunities

Allocate capital and effort based on expected value, risk, uncertainty, and opportunity cost.

These are priorities for consideration, not a rigid waterfall. Legal obligations, liquidity, risk, and business conditions may require concurrent funding or a different order.

Stocks and flows

Change in stock = inflow − outflow

Track critical stocks such as cash, available capacity, expertise, trust, asset health, and ecological resources. If consumption persistently exceeds replenishment, the stock declines even while current output looks strong.

07 // Dynamic stability

Adapt without oscillation

DAMPINGDo not make large changes in response to every small fluctuation.
PROPORTIONALITYMatch intervention size and speed to urgency, confidence, and reversibility.
PROTECTED SLACKMaintain spare capacity for shocks, exploration, and recovery.
TIME CONSTANTSAccount for different response speeds in information, people, systems, governance, and culture.
Rule: Never confuse the speed of issuing a change with the speed of achieving a stable new state.

Time-constant models are approximations. Real systems can be nonlinear, coupled, delayed, and subject to sudden shocks. Validate assumptions against observed behavior.

Every substantial intervention should have an expected result and a review condition. If it fails, revise or reverse it. If the framework itself does not improve outcomes, reduce risk, or preserve options, simplify the framework.

10 // Compact definition

Entropy Engine

A survival-oriented, self-realigning framework for reducing consequential uncertainty, selecting sufficient complexity, coordinating adaptive action, and circulating resources so that a system can continue creating value.

It operates under explicit safety, accountability, and viability constraints; preserves useful potential through repurposing where justified; and continuously tests its decisions against observed outcomes.

Design criterion
The system must become more capable of surviving, creating value, and adapting without undermining the capacities that make those outcomes possible.
