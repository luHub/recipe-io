00 // THE MANIFESTO

Software Engineering is an Art.

Software Engineering is often described as the application of mathematics, logic, engineering principles and technology to solve problems.

All of that is true. But it is not the whole story.

There is something that cannot be fully described through methodologies, certifications, frameworks, architectures or development processes.

There is a craft behind the profession. An instinct for structure. A curiosity for possibilities. An appreciation for elegance. A desire to understand, experiment, construct and refine.

Software Engineering is also an artistic pursuit.

You do not become a software engineer simply by learning how to write code. YOU BECOME ONE BY LEARNING TO CREATE WITH IT.
01 // THE CRAFT

You cannot be a painter if you hate painting.

Imagine someone who wants to be a painter but dislikes touching paint, mixing colors, experimenting with brushes, making mistakes or getting their hands dirty.

They might appreciate the finished paintings. They might understand color theory. They might know every painting technique. They might even operate the most sophisticated equipment.

But if they dislike the act of painting itself, something fundamental is missing.

The same applies to Software Engineering.

If you cannot appreciate the lines of code, the structure, the interactions, the problem-solving and the process of turning an abstract idea into something tangible, you might be pursuing the wrong profession.

You do not need to love every programming language, every framework or every task. You do not need to enjoy every technical responsibility.

But you should feel something when you create.

THE PAINTER
Mixing colors.
Experimenting with textures.
Finding composition.
Making something visible.
THE ENGINEER
Combining abstractions.
Experimenting with structures.
Finding solutions.
Making something executable.
The medium is different. The creative impulse is not.
02 // CURIOSITY IS A REQUIREMENT

The profession belongs to the curious.

Technology does not stand still.

Languages evolve. Paradigms change. Hardware develops. New abstractions emerge. Entire ways of building software become obsolete while new possibilities appear.

A software engineer should not approach these changes exclusively with fear, resistance or resentment.

Curiosity is not about blindly adopting everything new. It is about wanting to understand what has changed, why it exists, what it enables and whether it is useful.

You do not have to use every new technology. You should, however, remain interested in understanding it.

03 // TECHNOLOGY IS AN INSTRUMENT

Tools enhance the artist. They do not create the artist.

A painter can use digital brushes, automated color palettes, sophisticated canvases and artificial intelligence.

A musician can use synthesizers, digital audio workstations and generative tools.

A software engineer can use IDEs, compilers, frameworks, code generators, AI agents and automated development systems.

None of these tools diminish the creative process. They expand the available possibilities.

Technology should be embraced when it provides value. It should be questioned when it introduces unnecessary complexity or removes something that matters to the work.

TECHNOLOGY

Provides capabilities.

Accelerates execution.

Removes limitations.

Expands possibilities.

THE ENGINEER

Defines intentions.

Makes judgments.

Explores alternatives.

Gives direction to creation.

Technology can make you more capable. It cannot give you a reason to create.

Someone who does not enjoy discovering, understanding and creating with technology will probably find this profession increasingly miserable.

Not because technology is difficult, but because the source of satisfaction is missing.

04 // THE JOY OF CREATION

Enjoying the process of writing code is not a problem.

There is a particular pleasure in sitting down with an incomplete specification, an idea or even a vague problem and gradually discovering what the solution should become.

You start with an intention. You explore the problem. You discover missing requirements. You refine the specification. You experiment with structures. You write code. You observe the result. You change your mind. You improve the design.

Sometimes the act of programming is itself part of the thinking process.

The code is not simply the final implementation of an already completed thought. Writing it can help you discover what you actually meant in the first place.

05 // CREATIVE FREEDOM INCLUDES DELEGATION

You are also free not to write the code.

Here is an equally important point:

Enjoying the creative process does not make writing every line of code mandatory.

You may enjoy exploring a specification yourself. You may prefer to delegate its implementation. You may use an AI agent, another engineer, a code generator or an abstraction that takes care of the details.

You might want to focus on architecture, system behavior, product design, experimentation or something entirely different.

That is perfectly legitimate.

Delegation is not a betrayal of the craft. Automation is not a betrayal of the craft. Abstraction is not a betrayal of the craft.

The important distinction is understanding what you are delegating, why you are doing it and how you establish confidence in the resulting work.

PATH A // HANDS-ON

The Craftsperson

Enjoys working directly with the material. Explores specifications through implementation. Finds satisfaction in the details.

Creation through direct interaction.

PATH B // DELEGATION

The Orchestrator

Enjoys defining intentions, coordinating tools, delegating execution and validating results.

Creation through direction and composition.

Neither path is inherently superior. Both can involve creativity, intelligence, responsibility and craftsmanship.

The objective is not to maximize the number of lines you personally write. The objective is to create something meaningful and make sure it works.
06 // AGAINST DOCTRINE

This profession was never supposed to be a religion.

Over time, Software Engineering has accumulated methodologies, processes, conventions, certifications, frameworks, patterns and doctrines.

Many of them are useful. Some are necessary. Some solve real organizational and technical problems.

But none of them should become the purpose of the profession.

There is no universal development methodology that makes every engineer creative. There is no framework that guarantees good judgment. There is no programming language that automatically produces elegant solutions.

A technique is a tool. A methodology is a tool. A framework is a tool. An architecture is a tool. An AI agent is a tool.

They should serve the work, not become its masters.

WHAT WE SHOULD RESIST

01
Treating a programming language as an identity.
02
Confusing years of experience with curiosity.
03
Rejecting new tools simply because they challenge established habits.
04
Measuring engineering quality exclusively through process compliance.
05
Assuming that writing everything manually is inherently more valuable.
06
Confusing technical sophistication with creative achievement.
The moment the process becomes more important than the creation, we have forgotten why we started.
07 // A CREATIVE CONSTITUTION

Principles of the Craft

Not commandments. Not a methodology. Just principles worth defending.

01
Be curious.
Never stop exploring what technology makes possible.
02
Enjoy the material.
Appreciate the code, the abstractions, the systems and the act of creation.
03
Question everything.
Including your own assumptions and established practices.
04
Use every useful instrument.
Do not reject technology because it changes how the craft is performed.
05
Choose your level of involvement.
Write, delegate, automate or orchestrate. Choose according to the work and your intentions.
06
Take responsibility.
Creativity does not remove accountability. Verify what you create, regardless of who or what produced it.
07
Do not worship the tools.
The purpose is creation, not technological conformity.
08
Keep the joy alive.
Do not allow the machinery surrounding the profession to extinguish the curiosity that brought you into it.
08 // FINAL DECLARATION

SOFTWARE ENGINEERING IS AN ART.

Not because code is beautiful by definition, but because creating software requires imagination, exploration, experimentation, judgment and refinement.

You can enjoy writing every line. You can delegate the implementation. You can work close to the hardware or at the highest level of abstraction.

You can embrace AI, reject unnecessary complexity, change languages, reinvent your workflow and challenge established conventions.

There is no contradiction in any of that.


THERE IS NO SINGLE WAY TO CREATE.

There is only the willingness to explore, the freedom to experiment and the responsibility to make something that works.


KEEP CREATING.
